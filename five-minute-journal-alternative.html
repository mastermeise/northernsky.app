Northern Sky and the Five Minute Journal.

An honest comparison, for people looking for a Five Minute Journal alternative.

The Five Minute Journal is a good idea that has helped a lot of people: a short, structured morning instead of a blank page. If you own the paper one, or the app, and it works for you, keep it. This page is for the people who liked the idea and drifted off, and want to know how Northern Sky is different before they try another thing.

What the Five Minute Journal app does

Two sessions a day. In the morning: three things you're grateful for, three things that would make today great, and an affirmation. In the evening: three amazing things that happened, and how the day could have been better. Around that: prompts, quotes, photos, a day count, and a subscription in the range of forty dollars a year. It is a journal in the full sense, and it is designed to be filled in.

What Northern Sky does instead

One session, in the morning, about a minute. Two questions: what's the one thing you'll do today, and what are you thankful for. Then, the next morning, before anything else: yesterday you said you'd do this. Did you? Yes or no.

There is no evening entry, no affirmation, no prompt library, no quotes, no photos, and no day count. The commitment stays on screen all day under Stay focused on this. The Past keeps every morning: commitments with how they went, and gratitude on its own.

The differences that matter

Three things that would make today great, or one thing you'll do. Three wishes are easy to write and impossible to be asked about. One commitment is a decision, and it can be answered for the next day. That question is the reason Northern Sky exists.

How could today have been better, or did you do it. The Five Minute Journal's evening question is reflective and open. Northern Sky's is closed and asked the next morning, when the honest answer is easier to give. Neither is wrong; they produce different records. One is a record of reflections, the other a record of follow-through.

Amazing, or ordinary. A journal that asks for three amazing things every day is hard to fill on an ordinary Wednesday and harder on a bad one. Northern Sky asks what you're thankful for, once, and takes whatever you have.

A day count, or none. The Five Minute Journal shows how many days you've kept it up. Northern Sky keeps no count, on purpose; here is why.

Price. Northern Sky is $2.99 a month or $19.99 a year after a free week, and everything you wrote stays readable if you stop. Prices for the Five Minute Journal app are on its App Store page and change from time to time; check there.

Who should keep the Five Minute Journal

Anyone who wants prompts, photos, quotes and an evening session, or who loves the paper book and wants its app. It is a fuller journal than this, and it is meant to be.

Who might want this

Anyone who kept a five-minute journal for a month and stopped, who wants to start the day on purpose without writing much, and who would rather be asked one honest question tomorrow than three hopeful ones today.
